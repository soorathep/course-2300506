The survey offer

A market-research firm offers a demand survey for CNY 5 million. It reports either favorable (F) or unfavorable (U).

Its track record gives the chance of a favorable report in each true state. The survey is useful but not perfect: in one low-demand case out of five it still reports F.

The full tree, folded back

Each value after a report is an EMV with the posterior probabilities from the previous slide. 0.52 × 112.3 + 0.48 × 27.5 = 71.6.

What the survey is worth

EVSI = 71.6 − 56 = 15.6

EVSI is the expected value with the survey minus the expected value without it. Efficiency = EVSI / EVPI = 15.6 / 39 = 40%.

The survey is worth up to CNY 15.6 million. At CNY 5 million, buying it adds 10.6 million of expected value.

The survey has value only because an unfavorable report changes the choice from A to C. Information that cannot change a decision is worth nothing to that decision.

With a risk-averse board (R = 100), the survey still pays: its certainty equivalent is 36.4 against 34.8 without it, and after a favorable report the board picks B, not A.

Change the survey

Try P(F | state) = 0.5 in every state: the report then says nothing, and EVSI falls to zero.

A Pugh matrix compares each option with a reference, criterion by criterion: better (+), same (S) or worse (−). It screens quickly, but it treats every criterion as equally important.

Ratings in Sessions 2 and 3 are illustrative, written for the CellCo case, not an assessment of any product.

AHP in four steps

1 · Hierarchy Goal, then criteria, then alternatives.

2 · Pairwise comparisons For each pair of criteria: how much more important is one? Scale 1 (equal) to 9 (extreme).

3 · Weights Geometric mean of each row, normalized to sum to 1 (close to the principal eigenvector).

4 · Consistency CI = (λmax − n)/(n − 1), CR = CI / RI. Accept if CR < 0.10 (RI = 1.12 for n = 5).

Comparing two things at a time is easier than scoring five at once, and the consistency ratio tells you when the judgments contradict each other.

What we learned

A decision tree folds back from right to left: expected value at chance nodes, best branch at decision nodes.

Bayes’ theorem turns a survey’s track record into posterior probabilities. The first survey’s EVSI is CNY 15.6 million, 40% of EVPI, so it is worth its CNY 5 million price.

AHP turns pairwise judgments into weights and checks their consistency. CellCo’s three technologies score within 0.013, so the choice depends on whose weights the board adopts.
