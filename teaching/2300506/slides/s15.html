Question for today: CellCo’s cathode coating varies from coupon to coupon. Which variation should an operator react to, and can the gauge be trusted to tell?

The coating line

A slot die spreads cathode slurry on aluminum foil, which then dries in a long oven. The mass loading decides how much energy each cell stores.

Target: 20.0 mg/cm². Specification: 19.4 to 20.6 mg/cm².

Every hour, five coupons are punched across the web and weighed.

In Block 1, 93.3% of the cells that entered formation ended good. Today we look further upstream, at the coating line, where many defects start.

Common and special causes

Common-cause variation comes from many small sources always present: slurry viscosity, foil thickness, oven airflow. It is the process’s own noise. Reducing it needs a change to the system.

Special-cause variation comes from an identifiable event: a clogged die lip, a new slurry batch, a tension fault. It should be found and removed.

Two mistakes

Reacting to common-cause variation as if it were special (tampering) adds variation.

Ignoring a special cause lets a fault run.

A control chart separates the two, with limits set by the process’s own common-cause variation, not by the specification.

Tampering adds variation

Same noise in both runs. Adjusting after every coupon raises the standard deviation from 0.19 to 0.29 mg/cm²; in theory by a factor of √2.

Pareto of CellCo’s defect log

Coating defects (loading, streaks, pinholes, edges) are 36% of all defects; with slitting burrs, 60%.

A Pareto chart ranks problems so that effort goes to the few that matter. It counts defects, not their cost: if a weld defect costs ten times a burr, rank by cost as well.

Mass loading, the key coating variable, is today’s case. Before charting it, we must check the gauge.

Measurement system analysis

Observed variation = part-to-part variation + measurement variation.

σ²total = σ²part + σ²GRR, with σ²GRR = σ²repeatability + σ²reproducibility

Repeatability: one operator, same part, same gauge, repeated readings.

Reproducibility: different operators measuring the same parts.

%GRRVerdict
Below 10%Acceptable
10% to 30%Marginal: depends on use and cost
Above 30%Unacceptable: fix the gauge first

%GRR = σGRR / σtotal. Number of distinct categories, ndc = 1.41 σpart / σGRR, should be at least 5.

Explore a gauge study

CellCo’s coating gauge: repeatability 0.012, reproducibility 0.008, parts 0.170 mg/cm². %GRR 8.5%, ndc 16: fit for charting. Now shrink the part-to-part spread: the same gauge becomes useless.

Activity · Classroom Gage R&R

Individual, then whole class · 30 minutes · measurements and the measurement share

Measure the 10 printed line segments twice with your ruler, in millimeters to one decimal. Cover your first readings before the second round.

  1. Repeatability: for each segment, take the difference between your two readings. Average the 10 differences, then divide by 1.128.
  2. Reproducibility: enter your mean of all 20 readings in the class sheet. The standard deviation of the class means estimates it.
  3. Parts: the standard deviation of the 10 segment means over the whole class.
  4. Compute %GRR and ndc. Is a ruler fit to tell these segments apart?

Activity · What to look for

Resolution. A ruler reads to 0.5 or 1 mm. If the segments differ by only 1 to 2 mm, the ruler’s resolution alone pushes %GRR toward the marginal range. Rule of ten: resolution at most one tenth of the spread you need to see.

Reproducibility. Different starting points (zero mark or ruler edge) and viewing angles show up as differences between students’ means.

What it means for CellCo. A coating chart built on a poor gauge shows gauge noise as process variation: limits too wide, real shifts hidden.

Order of work. Check the gauge, then chart the process, then compute capability. Session 16 charts the coating data; Session 17 computes capability.

What we learned

Common-cause variation is the system’s noise; special causes are events to find. Tampering with a stable process adds variation.

Pareto charts aim effort: two defect types make 60% of CellCo’s defects, led by coating.

Check the gauge before charting. CellCo’s coating gauge has %GRR 8.5% and ndc 16: fit for use.

Session 16: control charts for the coating line, including charts for small drifts.
