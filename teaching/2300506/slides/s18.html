Four ways to count quality

Overall equipment effectiveness (OEE)Availability × performance × qualityComputed in the activity

In Block 1, the formation and aging stage passed 0.874 of cells first time but ended with 0.933 good after rework. The 5.9-point gap is the hidden factory: cells that pass only after rework, using test time and capacity that the final yield does not show.

OEE: one number, three losses

Availability = run time ÷ planned time. Lost to breakdowns, die changes, waiting for material.

Performance = actual output ÷ (run time × ideal rate). Lost to minor stops and reduced speed.

Quality = good first-pass output ÷ actual output. Lost to scrap and rework.

Breakdowns and scrap are logged; minor stops and slow running often are not. OEE makes the unlogged losses visible by working back from the ideal rate.

World-class OEE is often quoted near 85%. Compare a line with its own history rather than with that figure.

From yield to profit

The link to the LP. Coating is CellCo’s bottleneck. Any coating loss removed acts like extra coating hours, priced by the shadow price from Session 12: CNY 15,000 per hour up to 300 hours.

Where the yield is. Halving coating defects (FPY 0.972 to 0.986) is worth about CNY 52,000 a week. The mass-loading work in Session 17 removes only about 700 ppm; most coating losses are streaks, pinholes and edges. The Pareto, not one capability index, shows where the yield is.

What we learned

RTY counts first-pass quality across steps; the gap to final yield is the hidden factory of rework.

OEE exposes unlogged losses. CellCo’s largest single loss is minor stops and slow running, not scrap.

At the bottleneck, a yield gain is a capacity gain, priced by the LP’s shadow price.
