Question for today: CellCo’s coating machine wears out. When should it be maintained, and what does the right policy save?

From a chain to a decision

A Markov decision process adds choices to a Markov chain. In each state the planner picks an action, which sets both the cost and the transition probabilities.

A policy names one action for every state. Each policy is an ordinary Markov chain, so its steady state gives its long-run cost per shift.

Cost per shift = Σ πi × cost of the chosen action in state i. Maintaining only at major wear is cheapest, 53% below running to failure, and 8% below maintaining at any sign of wear.

Policy iteration

  1. Evaluate the current policy: solve g + hi = ci + Σj pij hj with hG = 0. Here g is the cost per shift and hi the extra cost of starting in state i.
  2. Improve: in each state, pick the action with the smallest c + Σ p h.
  3. Repeat until no action changes.

With four policies we can list them all. A real plant with ten wear levels and three actions has 310 = 59,049 policies; policy iteration still finds the best in a few steps.

What we learned

An MDP is a Markov chain whose transitions and costs depend on the action chosen in each state. Every policy has a steady state and a cost per shift.

For CellCo’s coating machine, PM only at major wear costs CNY 1,027 per shift, 53% less than running to failure.

The best policy depends on prices. Below a PM cost of about CNY 5,000, maintaining at any wear becomes best.
