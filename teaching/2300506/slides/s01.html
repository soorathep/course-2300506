Question for today: CellCo must add capacity before it knows the demand. Which option should it choose, and what does the answer depend on?

The case: CellCo adds capacity

CellCo makes LFP battery cells for energy storage and electric vehicles. The board wants 2 GWh per year more capacity.

Demand three years from now is uncertain: it may be low, medium or high. The board must commit before it knows which.

Each option is scored by its net present value (NPV) in CNY million over ten years.

A · New LFP line. Largest gain, largest loss.

B · Sodium-ion retrofit of an existing line. Cheaper, less upside.

C · Tolling contract with a partner plant. Small and safe.

D · Do nothing. NPV 0 in every state.

CellCo and every number in this course are fictional, written for teaching.

Four elements of a decision

Alternatives What you can choose.

A, B, C, D

States of nature What happens that you cannot control.

Low, medium, high demand

Payoffs The result of each alternative in each state.

NPV, CNY million

Criterion The rule that turns a table into a choice.

Break · 10 minutes

After the break we add probabilities. A market study gives low, medium and high demand probabilities of 0.3, 0.5 and 0.2.

Decisions under risk: expected monetary value

EMV(a) = Σs ps · payoff(a, s)

AlternativeLow, p = 0.3Medium, p = 0.5High, p = 0.2EMV
A · New LFP line−1208026056
B · Sodium-ion retrofit−406015048
C · Tolling contract10407037
D · Do nothing0000

EMV picks A at CNY 56 million. It assumes the board is risk-neutral: a loss of 120 weighs exactly as much as a gain of 120.

Expected opportunity loss

EOL is the expected regret: weight each regret by the state probability.

EOL(A) = 0.3 × 130 + 0.5 × 0 + 0.2 × 0 = 39

For every alternative, EMV + EOL = 95. Minimizing EOL and maximizing EMV always give the same choice.

What is a perfect forecast worth?

With a perfect forecast, CellCo picks the best option in each state: C if low, A if medium, A if high.

EV with perfect information = 0.3 × 10 + 0.5 × 80 + 0.2 × 260 = 95

EVPI = 95 − 56 = 39, the same number as the smallest EOL.

No market study, however good, is worth more than CNY 39 million to this decision. A study that costs more should not be bought.

Activity 2 · Price your own risk

On your own · 5 minutes · write before you click

You are CellCo’s CEO. Each gamble is a fair coin toss. Would you accept it for the company? Answer on paper first.

Answer all four on paper, then enter your answers here.

Rule of thumb: your risk tolerance R is about the largest X for which you accept “win X or lose X/2” on a coin toss.

Utility makes risk attitude explicit

U(x) = 1 − e−x/R      CE = −R · ln Σs ps e−xs/R

The certainty equivalent (CE) is the sure amount you would accept instead of the gamble. A smaller R bends the curve more, so the low-demand loss of A weighs more than its high-demand gain.

What we learned

Without probabilities, the criterion decides: maximin picks C, minimax regret picks B, and maximax picks A from the same table.

With probabilities, EMV picks A at CNY 56 million, and EVPI caps the value of any forecast at CNY 39 million.

Utility puts the board’s risk attitude into numbers. CellCo’s choice moves from A to B to C as its risk tolerance falls below 835 and then 180 million.
