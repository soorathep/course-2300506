Question for today: CellCo now runs the capacity chosen in Block 1. What should it make each week, and what is one more hour of coating worth?

From Block 1 to Block 2

Block 1 decided what to build and when it would be ready.

Block 2 runs the plant: Linear Programming plans what to make (today and Sessions 12 to 14); Statistical Quality Control keeps the coating line in control (Sessions 15 to 18).

You have already solved one LP. Crashing in Session 8 chose crash weeks to minimize cost subject to every path being at most 19 weeks.

The plant manager’s email

“We make two products on the same line. Energy-storage cells (E) earn CNY 30,000 per MWh; power cells (P) earn CNY 48,000 per MWh.

Coating is the problem: we have 240 coating hours a week. E needs 2 hours per MWh, P needs 3.

Formation has 150 hours a week. E needs 1.5 hours per MWh, P needs 1.

Customers will take at most 60 MWh of P a week; they take all the E we make.

What should we make?”

Three parts of every LP

Decision variables. E and P, weekly output in MWh.

Objective. Maximize contribution, in CNY thousand per week:

max 30E + 48P

Constraints.

2E + 3P ≤ 240 (coating hours)

1.5E + P ≤ 150 (formation hours)

P ≤ 60 (power-cell demand)

E, P ≥ 0

Checks before solving.

  • Units agree within each line: hours on both sides, CNY on the objective.
  • Each constraint names a resource someone can point to.
  • Linearity: doubling output doubles hours and contribution. No setup times, no volume discounts.
  • Divisibility: 30.5 MWh is allowed.

If these assumptions fail, Session 13 adds integer variables.

The graphical solution

Try a margin of P of 45: the contribution line runs parallel to the coating line, and every point on that edge is optimal.

Reading the corner

The optimum is E = 30, P = 60 MWh, contribution CNY 3.78 million a week.

Coating and power-cell demand are binding. Formation uses 1.5 × 30 + 60 = 105 of 150 hours: 45 hours idle.

Two lessons from the graph.

An LP optimum, if one exists, is always at a corner of the feasible region. Solver only needs to search corners.

Adding formation capacity is worthless today. Coating decides output until it reaches 300 hours; above that, formation becomes the limit.

Break · 10 minutes

After the break: a neighboring plant asks to rent one of CellCo’s coating hours. What is the lowest price CellCo should accept?

The dual: pricing the resources

Primal (the plant manager)

max 30E + 48P

2E + 3P ≤ 240

1.5E + P ≤ 150

P ≤ 60

Dual (a buyer of all resources)

min 240y1 + 150y2 + 60y3

2y1 + 1.5y2 ≥ 30 (E)

3y1 + y2 + y3 ≥ 48 (P)

y1, y2, y3 ≥ 0

A buyer who wants all of CellCo’s coating hours, formation hours and power-cell orders must pay at least what CellCo would earn by using them. The dual finds the cheapest such prices.

The dual solution

ResourceDual price, CNY thousandReading
Coating hour (y1)15One more coating hour adds CNY 15,000 a week
Formation hour (y2)045 hours are idle, so one more is worth nothing
MWh of power-cell demand (y3)3One more MWh of P orders adds CNY 3,000 a week

Strong duality. 240 × 15 + 150 × 0 + 60 × 3 = 3,780, equal to the primal optimum.

Complementary slackness. A resource with slack has dual price zero; a product made in positive amount earns exactly its resource cost: 2 × 15 = 30 for E, 3 × 15 + 3 = 48 for P.

Activity · Formulation relay and resource bid

Groups of 4 · 30 minutes · formulation sheet and bid

Part 1 (20 minutes). One member writes the variables, the next the objective, the next the constraints, the last solves graphically.

“Our pack shop makes home-storage batches (H, CNY 3,000 each) and telecom batches (T, CNY 5,000 each). Assembly has 40 hours a week: H takes 1 hour, T takes 2. Testing has 60 hours: H takes 3, T takes 2. Cell supply allows at most 18 telecom batches.”

Part 2 (10 minutes). Back to the cell line. Write your group’s minimum price for renting out one coating hour, with one sentence of reasoning. Then compare it with the dual price.

Activity · Answer

max 3H + 5T, CNY thousand

H + 2T ≤ 40 (assembly)

3H + 2T ≤ 60 (testing)

T ≤ 18, H, T ≥ 0

Corners: (20, 0) gives 60; (10, 15) gives 105; (4, 18) gives 102; (0, 18) gives 90.

Assembly and testing are binding. Their dual prices are 2.25 and 0.25 thousand per hour; the cell-supply limit is not binding.

For the coating hour: renting it out loses CNY 15,000 of contribution, so any price below 15,000 makes CellCo worse off. The dual price is the minimum rent, valid while coating stays between 180 and 300 hours.

What we learned

An LP has variables, an objective and constraints, and rests on linearity and divisibility. Check units line by line.

The optimum lies at a corner. For CellCo: E = 30, P = 60 MWh, CNY 3.78 million a week, with 45 formation hours idle.

The dual prices scarce resources. A coating hour is worth CNY 15,000; an idle formation hour is worth nothing.
