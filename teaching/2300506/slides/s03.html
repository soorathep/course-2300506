Three numbers hide the range

Question for today: what does the whole distribution of NPV tell us that a single expected value does not?

Monte Carlo simulation in five steps

1 · Model Write NPV as a formula of the uncertain inputs.

2 · Distributions Give each uncertain input a probability distribution, from data or expert judgment.

3 · Sample and repeat Draw one value of every input, compute NPV, and repeat 1,000 or more times.

4 · Summarize Report the mean, P10, P50, P90 and the probability of loss: the risk profile.

Step 5 is to question the result: which input drives the spread, and would a different but reasonable distribution change the decision?

In Excel: one row is one future

F2  U         =RAND()A2  demand    =IF(F2<0.5, 0.9+SQRT(F2*1.6*0.8), 2.5-SQRT((1-F2)*1.6*0.8))B2  price     =NORM.INV(RAND(), 380, 8)C2  cathode   =LOGNORM.INV(RAND(), LN(110), 0.08)D2  NPV       =-600 + 6.71*(MIN(2,A2)*(B2-C2-190) - 35)              copy row 2 down to row 1001, then:summary       =AVERAGE(D2:D1001)   =PERCENTILE.INC(D2:D1001,0.1)   =COUNTIF(D2:D1001,"<0")/1000

The triangular formula is the inverse of its cumulative distribution. (1.7 − 0.9)/(2.5 − 0.9) = 0.5 is the probability that demand falls below the most likely value. Press F9 to draw 1,000 new futures.

Activity 1 · Reference values

With 400,000 runs, option A has mean 57, P10 −208, P90 321 and P(loss) 40%.

With 1,000 runs, the standard deviation of NPV is about 201, so the standard error of the mean is 201/√1000 ≈ 6.4. Two honest pairs can differ by about ±13 in the mean.

Both matter. Fixing price and cathode cost cuts the SD of NPV from 201 to 149; fixing demand at 1.7 cuts it to 136. The margin is small, so its uncertainty is multiplied by the volume and by the annuity factor of 6.7.

The value of waiting: a real option

Staged strategy. Build 1 GWh now for CNY 330 million. After two years, CellCo knows its demand. If demand is at least a threshold T, add the second 1 GWh for another CNY 330 million.

Staging costs more in total (660 against 600), and the first two years sell at most 1 GWh. In return, CellCo spends the second 330 only in the futures where it pays.

NPVstaged = −330 + 1.783 × [min(1, D) × m − 20]
  + { if D ≥ T: −330 × 0.857 + 4.927 × [min(2, D) × m − 35]
     otherwise: 4.927 × [min(1, D) × m − 20] }

m = P − K − 190. 1.783 is the 2-year annuity factor, 4.927 covers years 3 to 10, and 0.857 discounts year 2.

The value of flexibility is E[NPV staged] − E[NPV commit]. A real option is the right, but not the obligation, to invest later.

Where simulation misleads

Correlated inputs. We drew price and demand independently. If price falls when demand falls (correlation 0.5), P10 of option A drops from −208 to −239. Ignoring correlation understated the downside.

Chosen distributions. The triangular range for demand came from three estimates by the sales team. Ask what evidence supports the minimum of 0.9, because the downside depends on it.

One draw per input. The model keeps price and demand fixed for ten years. A model with yearly changes would give a different spread. A simulation is only as good as its model.

A risk-neutral board expands when demand reaches about 1.9 GWh/yr.

A risk-averse board (R = 100) gains from every increase in T: at T = 2.4 it almost never expands, and its certainty equivalent is 37. For that board, the 1 GWh line alone is close to the safest choice.

Every staged strategy with T ≥ 1.7 beats committing now on mean, P10 and P(loss).

The best moment to expand depends on the board’s risk attitude. A cautious board values the option to wait so highly that it rarely uses the option to expand.

What we learned

Monte Carlo simulation turns a model and input distributions into a full risk profile. Option A’s mean NPV of 57 hides a 40% chance of loss.

With 1,000 runs, the mean carries a standard error of about 6. Report the run count, and do not read meaning into differences smaller than that.

Building in stages is a real option. Expanding only if demand reaches 1.9 GWh/yr adds about 22 million of expected NPV and halves the chance of loss.
